# Jev scam-signal post-training: dataset shortlist and experiment plan

Checked 2026-10-08. The URLs below opened as dataset landing pages. This check does **not** establish that every file can be downloaded without an account, that its license permits the intended use, or that its labels are correct. The local `verdict-151m/` bundle contains an ONNX inference model; the trainable upstream checkpoint is [heman10x/rlcd-modernbert-151m](https://huggingface.co/heman10x/rlcd-modernbert-151m/tree/main), which lists `model.safetensors`.

## Candidate datasets

| Source | What it offers | Use and limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [ICFD-31k](https://huggingface.co/datasets/rishia2220/icfd-31k) | 31,000 synthetic English/Hinglish phone-style calls and over 1.1 million cumulative streaming chunks with conversation IDs. | Strongest starting point for prefix training. Its `other` license and defensive-use terms need review; a call's final verdict must not be copied to every early prefix as if evidence is already present. |
| [Phone Scam Dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/menaattia/phone-scam-dataset) | About 4,000 short caller/receiver dialogues labeled scam or legitimate, including bank and dental examples. | Good small pilot and hard-negative source. Review undocumented provenance and licensing; binary labels do not supply Jev's ten signal labels. |
| [BothBosu scam-dialogue](https://huggingface.co/datasets/BothBosu/scam-dialogue) | 1,600 synthetic English multi-turn calls, balanced across four scam and four benign types; Apache 2.0. | Useful starter data, but narrow templates make random-row test scores unreliable. |
| [BothBosu multi-agent scam conversation](https://huggingface.co/datasets/BothBosu/multi-agent-scam-conversation) | 1,600 synthetic, longer scam/benign dialogues with receiver personalities; Apache 2.0. | Use as a variant of the BothBosu family, not an independent test corpus. |
| [BothBosu single-agent scam conversations](https://huggingface.co/datasets/BothBosu/single-agent-scam-conversations) | 1,600 synthetic dialogues with marked caller/receiver turns; Apache 2.0. | Useful for role-sensitive labels; deduplicate against the other BothBosu sets before splitting. |
| [Phone Scam Detection Synthetic](https://huggingface.co/datasets/shakeleoatmeal/phone-scam-detection-synthetic) | 1,800 synthetic scam/legitimate dialogues with length and subtlety variants; MIT. | Use for robustness training, not real-call accuracy claims. |
| [Scam and Non-Scam Call Conversation Dataset](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/teeconnie/scam-and-non-scam-call-conversation-dataset/versions/1) | 400 scam and 400 benign English call transcripts, partly collected and augmented from reported experiences. | Valuable cross-source evaluation candidate. CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 limits reuse; confirm training rights before using it for fine-tuning. |
| [Indian Scam Communication Dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ysangam/Indian_Cyber_Scam_PhoneCall_Hinglish_Dataset) | 10,000 scam/benign texts spanning calls, SMS, messaging, English, Hindi, and Hinglish; Apache 2.0. | Filter to call records and target languages; it is mixed-channel and often short. Avoid counting its mirrors as new data. |
| [SCAMBENCH](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Shouninger/ScamBench) | 1,836 victim-reported scam messages, 1,834 non-scam messages, persuasion/red-flag annotations, and subtle rewrites. | Strong signal-taxonomy and hard-negative material, but not call dialogue. Gated and CC BY-NC 4.0. |
| [ScamBench Training Corpus](https://huggingface.co/datasets/shaw/scambench-training) | 37,423 multilingual agent-safety examples with social-engineering categories and legitimate counterparts. | Auxiliary tactic supervision only: many records are not phone calls. Audit source lineage and noisy synthetic labels. |
| [AIxBlock English call-center transcripts](https://huggingface.co/datasets/AIxBlock/92k-real-world-call-center-scripts-english) | 91,706 claimed PII-redacted real English customer-service transcripts with ASR information; CC BY-NC 4.0. | Candidate benign/hard-negative domain data. No scam labels; inspect archives, consent/provenance, and residual PII before use. |
| [BANKING77](https://huggingface.co/datasets/PolyAI/banking77) | 13,083 banking customer questions across 77 legitimate intents; CC BY 4.0. | Bank-language negatives/intent pretraining only. Single queries are not full calls, and the original Hub loader requires special handling. |
| [DailyDialog](https://huggingface.co/datasets/roskoN/dailydialog) | 13,118 human-written everyday dialogues; original dataset is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. | Benign conversational diversity; weak match for telephone fraud and requires label review. |
| [TeleAntiFraud](https://huggingface.co/datasets/JimmyMa99/TeleAntiFraud) | Chinese telecom-fraud audio/text research dataset with scenario and fraud annotations. | Multilingual expansion only; files require accepting access conditions. Do not mix directly into an English-only model. |
| [FraudAlign-MCS](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ggirishg/MultiFraudAlign) | 28,708 synthetic fraud-only dialogues across English, Hindi, Korean, and Hinglish with caller/callee turns and tactic fields. | Potential signal positives, but manually gated, fraud-only, and marked `other` license; add independent benign calls. |

The [real scam/spam call corpus described by Scam AI](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29528) is a promising later evaluation source. The paper describes a 1,000-call public subset, but its release does not yet give a stable direct dataset URL; the full corpus requires a data-use agreement. The public subset excludes legitimate calls, so it cannot measure false alarms by itself.

## Fine-tuning sequence

1. **Freeze the target.** Jev in this app answers ten caller-behavior questions over a bounded rolling transcript, with yes/no/insufficient-evidence outcomes. Keep the exact shipped propositions and prompt rendering in `ios/ScamTranscript/Resources/JevSignals.json` and `backend/jev-prototype/jev_scam_signals.py`. A binary scam label cannot be silently used as a label for all ten questions.
2. **Build a licensed, reviewed corpus.** Import the direct call datasets, keep source, license, original ID, script/campaign ID, caller role, language, and full turn boundaries. Deduplicate exact and near-duplicate scripts across sources. Redact PII. Human-label every Jev signal on transcript prefixes, including legitimate bank, medical, IT, family, and sales calls that mention sensitive topics. Mark absent evidence as *insufficient*, not automatically *false*.
3. **Split before augmentation.** Keep every prefix, paraphrase, ASR variant, call, caller, and script family in one split. Reserve an untouched real-call/cross-source test set; synthetic data can enlarge training but must not be the only evidence for deployment. Check class coverage per signal and source before fitting.
4. **Measure the current checkpoint.** Run the existing ONNX bundle with the exact iOS prompts on the frozen evaluation suite. Report precision/recall, macro-F1 and PR-AUC per signal, false alerts on legitimate calls, early-turn recall, and abstention. Include the code-request scam versus dentist-rescheduling contrast.
5. **Train a compatible checkpoint.** Start from the upstream `model.safetensors`, not `model.onnx`. Fine-tune the existing GLiClass/ModernBERT three-choice interface with cross-entropy plus Brier loss, balanced batches, hard negatives, and early stopping on validation NLL. Compare against the unchanged checkpoint and a small rule/TF-IDF baseline. Preserve the 512-token input budget and caller-role formatting.
6. **Calibrate rather than inflate confidence.** Fit temperature or another simple calibrator on a separate validation set for each signal; choose alert thresholds there and lock them. Report Brier score, NLL, ECE, reliability plots, abstention coverage, and false-positive rates on the untouched test set. More extreme probabilities are an improvement only when observed correctness supports them.
7. **Export and gate deployment.** Export the selected weights to ONNX, check Python/iOS parity on golden examples, and benchmark latency, memory, and noisy ASR on the intended device. Replace the app bundle only if held-out signal quality and calibration improve without unacceptable legitimate-call alerts. Keep the old bundle as a rollback artifact.

## Current execution status

The public files for Phone Scam Dataset, BothBosu scam-dialogue, Phone Scam Detection Synthetic, ICFD-31k, the Indian Cyber Scam Hinglish dataset, the two other BothBosu variants, and Kaggle's short-call dataset were downloaded locally without an account or payment. The ICFD-31k download passed its published SHA-256 checksums. The trainable Jev `model.safetensors` was downloaded, and a 151M-parameter checkpoint was trained on 3,600 balanced synthetic or synthetic-style calls. A second caller-only pass produced a separate short-call variant. Both ONNX exports passed PyTorch parity checks. See [the run record](backend/jev-posttrain/README.md) for exact splits, metrics, limitations, commands, and artifact paths.

The current iOS ten-signal scorer has not been replaced. Whole-call scam labels do not validate its individual behavior questions; a qualitative probe of the post-trained checkpoints marked every behavior as present on a code-request scam, including behaviors absent from that text. No held-out real-call ten-signal corpus is available. The two additional BothBosu variants were downloaded after the first run but not used for training because they belong to the same source family. Kaggle was reserved for evaluation because of its CC BY-NC-ND license. No paid options or account requirements were necessary for the completed training experiment.
